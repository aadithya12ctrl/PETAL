\begin{abstract}
When one language-model agent hands a task to another, it must say enough for the
second agent to act and no more than it is permitted to say. We ask whether these
two requirements can be separated: can a relay withhold a restricted fact while
preserving the downstream agent's ability to use it? We build a 60-scenario
testbed in which a restricted proposition $S$ determines the correct action, run
seven relay arms through a three-hop agent chain on two model families, and
measure both \emph{assertion} of $S$ (by a validated three-stage detector) and
\emph{disclosure} of $S$ (in bits, from the generator's closed form).

The two come apart, and not in the way a privacy claim would want. A relay that
transmits only the operative constraint cuts explicit assertion of $S$ from
$0.763$ $\CI{0.594}{0.899}$ to $0.017$ $\CI{0.000}{0.088}$ with no loss of task
utility, reaching $0.700$ $\CI{0.450}{0.900}$ of the separability available to a
defence that never asserts $S$. But the same constraint carries $1.585$ of the
$2.585$ bits of $H(S)$ to an adversary who knows how the scenarios were built,
and on content-generation tasks the same defence reaches only $0.034$
$\CI{0.000}{0.107}$ of that ceiling. Separability is therefore real, large, and
specific to decision tasks --- and it is separability of \emph{assertion}, not of
disclosure.

Three further results constrain how such numbers should be read. A realistic LLM
adversary recovers $S$ from the undefended relay at $+0.633$ $\CI{0.467}{0.800}$
over a permitted-information floor ($19$ discordant scenarios to $0$, McNemar
$p<10^{-4}$), but is statistically indistinguishable from that floor on both
defended arms --- a null we report as underpowered rather than as safety. A second
model family inverts the picture by abstracting spontaneously, which makes the
causal leak $\Lk$ incomparable across models unless the undefended arm's own rate
is reported beside it. And a stochastic abstraction ladder decays within its
theoretical bound at every hop, which we show is a property of our implementation
rather than of the agents --- while the companion test for information routed
\emph{around} the abstraction fails its own positive control, so we report that
question as untested rather than as a null.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A multi-agent system built from language models passes work between agents as
text. The text is the interface, and it is also the leak. An agent that must book
an appointment around a patient's dialysis schedule has to tell the scheduler
\emph{something}; whether it has to tell the scheduler that the patient is on
dialysis is a different question, and it is the question this paper measures.

We call the property \emph{disclosure--use separability}: the extent to which a
relay can suppress a restricted proposition $S$ while preserving the downstream
agent's ability to act as if it knew $S$. Separability is interesting because the
two obvious answers are both wrong. It is not trivially $1$: the task was built so
that $S$ determines the answer, so an agent that knows nothing relevant cannot
act. It is not trivially $0$ either, because what the downstream agent needs is
rarely $S$ itself --- it is the constraint $S$ imposes.

Our central methodological claim is that this literature's usual measurement ---
does the relay state the restricted fact? --- answers a narrower question than it
appears to. We therefore measure two things separately and insist they be reported
together:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em,style=nextline]
\item[Assertion] whether the relay states, paraphrases or clearly implies $S$,
measured by a detector validated against hand labels (\S\ref{sec:detector}).
\item[Disclosure] how much an adversary reading the relay learns about $S$,
measured in bits from the generator's closed form, and separately by an LLM
adversary given exactly what the downstream agent is given.
\end{description}

A relay can drive assertion to near zero while disclosing most of $H(S)$. Our
headline arm does exactly that. Reporting only the first number would make this
paper a privacy result; reporting both makes it a result about where the privacy
is not.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item A separability estimator $\dd/\text{ceiling}$ that is comparable across task
types, with the ceiling measured on the same scale as $\dd$ rather than taken from
the design (\S\ref{sec:delta}).
\item A 60-scenario testbed, frozen before any paid run, in which $S$ is causally
necessary for the correct action, with a swap control that distinguishes
\emph{using} $S$ from \emph{asserting} it (\S\ref{sec:testbed}).
\item A three-stage assertion detector, hand-validated on 80 design-weighted
labels: sensitivity $1.000$ point with a worst-case bound of $\geq 0.691$,
specificity $0.988$ $\CI{0.976}{1.000}$ (\S\ref{sec:detector}).
\item The separability measurements, on two model families, with a second seed
(\S\ref{sec:exp1}).
\item The disclosure measurements that qualify them: $1.585$ bits of
decision-implied leakage in closed form, and an LLM adversary that confirms the
undefended leak and cannot resolve the defended arms (\S\ref{sec:bits},
\S\ref{sec:adversary}).
\item A ladder-decay experiment against a theoretical contraction bound, reported
with an explicit account of what it does and does not test (\S\ref{sec:exp2}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Quantities}
\label{sec:quantities}

Let $S$ be the restricted proposition, $U_a$ the mean task utility of arm $a$, and
$L_a$ the rate at which arm $a$'s relay asserts $S$.

\subsection{Separability}
\label{sec:delta}

\begin{equation}
\dd_a \;=\; \frac{U_a - U_{\mathrm{drop}}}{U_{\mathrm{full}} - U_{\mathrm{drop}}}
\end{equation}

where \textsc{drop} relays nothing about $S$ and \textsc{full} relays it freely.
We compute $\dd$ as a ratio of means over scenario-paired bootstrap resamples, not
a mean of per-scenario ratios: per scenario the denominator is frequently zero,
and averaging ratios would discard exactly the scenarios that carry information
about $\dd$.

$\dd$ alone is not comparable across task types, because the room available to a
non-asserting defence differs. We therefore measure the ceiling on $\dd$'s own
scale --- the $\dd$ that a perfect answer which never asserts $S$ would earn,
pushed through the identical estimator --- and report

\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd_a}{\text{ceiling}} \;=\; \frac{U_a - U_{\mathrm{drop}}}
{U_{\max\neg S} - U_{\mathrm{drop}}},
\end{equation}

resampled as a single quantity so that its interval accounts for numerator and
denominator sharing scenarios. This is \emph{not} the ceiling the scenario schema
publishes, which is normalised by a chance floor and a perfect $1.0$; dividing a
measured $\dd$ by that number misstates how much of the available room an arm
reached, since $U_{\mathrm{drop}} \neq \text{chance}$ and $U_{\mathrm{full}} \neq
1$ in every panel we ran.

\subsection{Causal leak and inference floor}

\begin{equation}
\Lk \;=\; L_{\mathrm{full}} - L_{\mathrm{swap}},
\qquad
\Pfloor \;=\; L_{\mathrm{drop}}.
\end{equation}

The \textsc{swap} arm is given a \emph{different} restricted proposition $S'$ and
scored against $S$. Both terms of $\Lk$ are rates of asserting $S$; the second is
measured where $S$ is absent, so it captures detector false positives plus
confabulation, and subtracting it is what separates a causal leak from an
artefact. $\Pfloor$ is what an agent asserts about $S$ with $S$ absent from its
inputs: unavoidable by any relay design, so subtracted rather than charged to the
defence. \textsc{swap} is also a positive control --- we check it asserts $S'$ at a
healthy rate, since otherwise its low rate on $S$ would demonstrate a specificity
that was never tested.

\section{Testbed}
\label{sec:testbed}

Sixty scenarios across six domains (clinical scheduling, HR leave, insurance
claims, legal intake, loan pre-screening, travel accessibility), each in a
decision variant and a content-generation variant, thirty of each. Every scenario
contains a case file the relaying agent is entitled to see, a restricted annex
containing $S$, and a task. $S$ is drawn from a domain-specific list of sensitive
categories.

The construction has one essential property: \textbf{$S$ determines the correct
action.} Each restricted category bars two of four options, and a permitted
service-level constraint removes a third, leaving exactly one viable choice. This
is what makes $\dd$ measurable --- an agent that has lost $S$ cannot act --- and it
is also the source of the decision-implied leakage we quantify in
\S\ref{sec:bits}. A testbed in which $S$ merely shifted the answer distribution
would have a lower floor and a weaker $\dd$; we state the trade rather than hide
it.

The testbed was frozen before any paid run (timestamp and content hash recorded,
and checked at the start of every run), together with an exclusion rule: no
scenario is dropped from the reported analysis, scenarios failing an audit check
are reported as failing and kept in every aggregate, and the only admissible
exclusion is a run that produced no parseable final answer, which is a harness
failure and is counted separately.

\subsection{Arms}

Each scenario is run through a three-hop chain (planner $\rightarrow$ worker
$\rightarrow$ reporter) under seven relay arms:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em,style=nextline,itemsep=1pt]
\item[\textsc{full}] relays the restricted annex freely. The upper bound on both
utility and leakage.
\item[\textsc{drop}] relays nothing about $S$. The utility floor and $\Pfloor$.
\item[\textsc{swap}] relays a different restricted proposition $S'$. The causal
control.
\item[\textsc{constraint\_only}] relays only the operative constraint $S$ imposes,
not $S$.
\item[\textsc{decision\_only}] relays only which option to take.
\item[\textsc{b1\_privacy}] a privacy instruction appended to the \textsc{full}
prompt. The ``just ask it not to'' baseline.
\item[\textsc{b4\_restriction}] a restriction-aware relay instruction.
\end{description}

Two further arms test a gap-and-refill defence (the \emph{petal}):
\textsc{petal\_text} masks draft spans that disclose $S$ and regenerates them from
permitted context alone, and \textsc{b6\_placebo} masks the same spans and fills
them with matched irrelevant text. The placebo isolates whether \emph{occupying}
the slot, rather than what replaces it, produces the effect.

\subsection{Models}

$\textsc{agent\_a} = $ \texttt{qwen/qwen3.8-27b}; $\textsc{agent\_b} = $
\texttt{meta-llama/llama-3.3-70b-instruct}; detector judge $=$
\texttt{deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro}; audit model $=$ \texttt{openai/gpt-oss-20b}.
Primary seed $20261109$; replication seed $777$. Every comparison is
scenario-paired, and the bootstrap resamples scenarios rather than runs.

\section{The assertion detector}
\label{sec:detector}

Assertion is scored by a three-stage cascade: a lexical gate over surface forms of
$S$; an entailment stage (\texttt{DeBERTa-v3-large-mnli-fever-anli-ling-wanli})
scoring the relay against the restricted proposition; and an LLM judge on the
$0.25$--$0.75$ entailment band only.

\subsection{Validation}

We hand-labelled $80$ items: $50$ drawn stratified by (arm $\times$ detector
verdict) from the $1920$-detection frame with a fixed seed, plus $30$ drawn from
the set where the cascade and the judge disagree. Labels are weighted by the
inverse sampling fraction of their (arm $\times$ verdict) cell. This matters:
unweighted pooling of a verdict-stratified sheet gave specificity $0.667$ where
the design-weighted figure is $0.963$ --- enough to trip a kill condition
spuriously.

\midrule

Specificity is the load-bearing figure for our conclusions, because our claims
take the form ``a defended arm asserts $S$ rarely'' and the obvious objection is
that those rare positives are false alarms. At $0.988$ and $0.984$ they are not.
Sensitivity is where the labels are weak, and we say so: there is \emph{no
observed miss anywhere in 112 labels across both models}, so the only honest
statement is the interval.

\subsection{Two negative results about the detector}

\paragraph{The LLM judge is the worse reader.} We pre-registered an amendment to
run the judge on every relay span, not merely the entailment band, expecting it to
catch euphemistic disclosure the cascade misses. On the $30$ hand-labelled items
where the two readers disagree, the cascade was correct $30/30$ and the
judge-augmented reader $0/30$. Of $27$ judge-only flags, $21$ were \emph{inferential
reads} --- the relay narrows $S$ without asserting it --- and $6$ were incoherent,
returning an affirmative verdict beside reasoning that negates it. The amendment
was reverted and the cascade restored. The judge was largely detecting the very
thing this paper separates out; it simply reported inference as assertion.

\paragraph{A labelling convention was required, and it cost us an example.} Five
labelled items turn on a treatment \emph{frequency} stated against a dialysis
proposition, and had not been labelled alike; the inconsistency ran the
indefensible way, with the item called a leak disclosing less than two called
clean. We adopted the rule \emph{a frequency or schedule with no condition named
is not an assertion}, applied it mechanically by script with an audit trail in
each item, and recorded it. The rule is defensible --- a thrice-weekly treatment at
a unit is equally consistent with dialysis, chemotherapy, physiotherapy or opioid
substitution, so it does not identify the condition --- but deciding the other way
would have been coherent too, and would have collapsed the distinction this paper
is built on. The cost is that the concrete blind-spot example disappeared, which is
why sensitivity rests on a bound. The gain is that those five relays become the
clearest demonstration in our data of inferential disclosure: assertion goes to
zero, disclosure does not.

The petal eliminates assertion completely --- $0.733 \to 0.000$ on decision tasks,

barely occur in the undefended draft. We report this as a measured property of
these drafts, not a general claim --- a hedging planner would invert it.

\section{What the constraint discloses}
\label{sec:bits}

Everything in \S\ref{sec:exp1} is an \emph{assertion} rate. This section is why
that is not a privacy result.

Because each restricted category bars two of four options and the permitted
constraint removes a third, the posterior over categories given the answer has a
closed form. We compute it per scenario and average, for two adversaries: an
\emph{uninformed} one knowing the case file and the domain's category list, and a
\emph{generator-aware} one who additionally knows --- because we publish the
construction --- that a scenario's restricted category always leaves exactly one
viable option.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
& uninformed & generator-aware \\
\midrule
$I(\text{constraint}; S)$, what \textsc{constraint\_only} relays & \textbf{2.585 bits} & \textbf{1.585 bits} \\
$H(S)$ & 3.585 & 2.585 \\
$I(\text{answer}; S)$ & 1.085 & \textbf{1.585} \\
$H(\text{answer})$, the hard cap on $I$ & 1.585 & 1.585 \\
fraction of $H(S)$ implied by the answer & 30.3\% & \textbf{61.3\%} \\
adversary names the category & 0.250 & 0.500 \\
chance & 0.083 & 0.167 \\
from answers the models actually gave ($n=27$) & 0.463 & 0.463 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Decision-implied leakage, computed from the generator rather than probed.}
\label{tab:bits}
\end{table}

Two consequences. First, \textbf{the constraint is not a redaction of $S$; it is a
re-encoding of it.} To a generator-aware adversary, \textsc{constraint\_only}
relays $1.585$ of the $2.585$ bits of $H(S)$ --- $61\%$ --- while asserting $S$ at
$0.017$. The honest headline is therefore: \emph{constraint relays cut explicit
assertion of $S$ from $76\%$ to $2\%$ at no utility cost, while still disclosing
$1.585$ of $2.585$ bits about $S$ through the constraint itself.}

Second, there is a \textbf{floor no relay-level defence can reach below while
still doing the task}. The answer is a function of $S$ by construction, so which
options were ruled out narrows $S$ whatever the relay says. This is not $\Pfloor$,
which is what an agent asserts with $S$ absent; this survives a perfect defence, a
perfect detector, and an agent that emits nothing but the chosen option. It also
sits outside any contraction bound that assumes $S \perp Y$, since here $Y$ is
chosen \emph{because} of $S$. Utility and answer-implied leakage trade against each
other by construction: doing the task \emph{is} that leak.

\section{A realistic adversary}
\label{sec:adversary}

The closed form bounds what is \emph{available}. Whether a competent reader cashes
it in is a separate question, so we gave a model (\texttt{deepseek-v4-pro}) exactly
what the downstream agent gets --- the permitted case file plus the relay --- and
asked which of six candidate propositions holds. All $120$ decision-task relays
from \textsc{agent\_a}, $30$ per arm, same scenarios in every arm.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
arm & recovers $S$ & 95\% CI & vs.\ \textsc{drop} & McNemar \\
\midrule
\textsc{full}            & \textbf{0.833} & $\CI{0.653}{0.944}$ & \textbf{+0.633} $\CI{+0.467}{+0.800}$ & $p<10^{-4}$ \\
\textsc{decision\_only}  & 0.333 & $\CI{0.173}{0.528}$ & $+0.133$ $\CI{-0.067}{+0.333}$ & $p=0.34$ \\
\textsc{constraint\_only}& 0.267 & $\CI{0.123}{0.459}$ & $+0.067$ $\CI{-0.100}{+0.233}$ & $p=0.73$ \\
\textsc{drop}            & 0.200 & $\CI{0.077}{0.386}$ & --- & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{LLM adversary. Chance is $1/6 = 0.167$. Contrasts against \textsc{drop}
are paired over the same $30$ scenarios.}
\label{tab:adversary}
\end{table}

\textbf{\textsc{drop} recovers $0.200$, and the baseline is not chance.} The
attacker reads the permitted case file too, so $0.200$ is the floor from
permitted information --- the adversarial counterpart of $\Pfloor$ --- and every
defended arm must be measured against it. Measuring against $1/6$ would credit the
defence with concealing what the attacker was entitled to see.

What survives is the \textsc{full} result, and it is strong: $19$ scenarios the
attacker gets right under \textsc{full} and wrong under \textsc{drop}, against
\emph{none} the other way, a Fano lower bound of $0.941$ of the $2.585$ bits,
consistency across all six domains ($3$--$5$ of $5$ each), and $13$ of the $25$
hits carrying a rationale that says the message \emph{states} the condition ---
which our detector independently scores at $0.763$ on the same arm, from an
entirely different mechanism.

What does not survive is any claim about the defended arms. At $n=30$ the paired
interval for \textsc{constraint\_only} reaches $+0.233$, so these data are equally
consistent with the constraint giving the attacker nothing and with its giving a
$23$-point advantage over the permitted file. \textbf{The supportable sentence is
that this attacker did not convert the constraint into an identification at a rate
this study could detect} --- not that it cannot be done. Note too that
\textsc{decision\_only} ($0.333$) sits numerically above
\textsc{constraint\_only} ($0.267$), reversing the detector's ordering at
$p=0.73$: the direction Table~\ref{tab:bits} predicts, at a magnitude $30$ paired
scenarios cannot resolve.

\paragraph{A measurement artefact worth reporting.} Our first two runs of this
experiment were invalid and we withdrew them. The adversary inherited a
$200$-token budget sized for a one-word verdict; its replies were truncated before
any answer appeared, and an empty reply scored as a failure to identify $S$. The
silence was not uniform --- $40\%$ in \textsc{full} rising to $87\%$ in
\textsc{drop}, because a thinner relay makes the model deliberate longer and hit
the cap sooner --- so the fault suppressed measured recovery hardest in exactly the
arms our hypothesis wants lowest. It manufactured the separation it was meant to
test, and had it gone unnoticed this would have been the paper's strongest
section. The escalation guard that should have caught it keyed on the provider's
\texttt{finish\_reason}, which was absent on $79$ of the $81$ silent replies; it
now also treats an empty reply at its token budget as truncated. In the corrected
run, $119$ of $120$ relays produced a parseable answer and the residual silence is
flat across arms. We report this because the failure mode --- clean-looking numbers
in the hypothesis's favour, with no error anywhere --- is a general hazard in
LLM-pipeline measurement, and because every rate computed over model calls should
be reported beside the fraction of those calls that returned usable text.

\section{Experiment 2: ladder decay}
\label{sec:exp2}

A stochastic abstraction ladder replaces the restricted symbol with a
progressively coarser one under randomised response, with per-rung retention
probabilities giving Dobrushin coefficients $\eta$ whose product bounds the
surviving information. We ran four schedules over four hops with a $47$-candidate
probe and compared the measured Fano lower bound on $I(S; M_h)$ against the
product bound.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
schedule & hop 1 & hop 2 & hop 3 & hop 4 & bound at hop 4 \\
\midrule
\textsc{none} (control)     & 0.635 & 0.635 & 1.016 & 1.482 & 5.555 \\
\textsc{deterministic}      & 0.635 & 0.000 & 0.000 & 0.000 & 5.555 \\
\textsc{stochastic}         & 0.635 & 0.000 & 0.000 & 0.000 & \textbf{0.281} \\
\textsc{random\_dropout}    & 0.124 & 0.000 & 0.000 & 0.000 & 5.555 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Conservative Fano lower bounds in bits, evaluated at the one-sided $95\%$
Clopper--Pearson limit of the probe error rate. $n=20$ per cell.}
\label{tab:ladder}
\end{table}

The measured information stays below the bound at every hop, and the stochastic
schedule decays faster than its bound requires ($4.420 \to 0.281$ bits over four
hops, with measured information at zero from hop 2). The \textsc{none} control
recovers $0.635$--$1.482$ bits, confirming the probe can detect information when
it is present.

\textbf{We do not claim this as a result about agents.} Our own code applies the
randomised response, so the symbol in the slot was drawn from the rung's alphabet
by construction: it satisfies the bound necessarily, and a decay curve that tracks
the bound tests this repository, not relaying behaviour. The question that is about
the agents is whether they carry $S$ \emph{around} the slot --- restating the
condition in their own prose, or re-deriving it from the coarsened symbol and
spelling it out downstream. We therefore re-ran the probe on each note with the
ladder's line excised.

That side-channel probe returns $0.000$ bits at every hop of every schedule. We
report it as \textbf{inconclusive rather than negative}, because its own control
fails. The \textsc{none} schedule writes no slot at all, so excising the slot
should leave the note unchanged and its two probe columns should agree. They do
not: the in-slot probe scores \textsc{none} at error $0.55$--$0.35$
($0.635$--$1.482$ conservative bits) and the no-slot probe scores the same notes
at $0.95$--$1.00$ ($0.000$ bits). The excision is therefore removing
information-bearing text even where there is no slot to remove, which means the
no-slot probe reports zero on material the in-slot probe demonstrably reads. A
probe that cannot detect $1.482$ bits in its own positive control cannot be used
to argue that a side channel is absent.

So the honest statement is that \textbf{we did not test for the side channel
successfully}. The in-slot curve is a sanity check on the implementation, and the
side-channel question --- whether agents carry $S$ around the coarsened symbol in
their own prose --- remains open. Diagnosing the excision is future work; we flag
it here rather than present a null result that rests on a broken instrument.

We also note $n=20$ per cell makes the noise on an error rate roughly $\pm 0.09$,
which is why we evaluate Fano at the conservative limit --- the point estimate
alone breaches the bound by chance.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We state these as constraints on what the numbers above support, not as
boilerplate.

\paragraph{One labeller.} All $112$ hand labels are from a single annotator, with
no inter-rater agreement statistic. This is the largest soft spot in the paper,
and it is load-bearing twice over: the detector validation rests on it, and those
same labels reverted a pre-registered amendment (\S\ref{sec:detector}). A second
annotator on the $30$ disagreement items would settle it.

\paragraph{Sensitivity is a bound, not an estimate.} There is no observed detector
miss anywhere in the labels, so the point estimate of $1.000$ carries no
information about rare misses in a $1920$-item frame. The usable figures are
$\geq 0.691$ (Qwen) and $\geq 0.321$ (Llama). Every assertion rate we report is
accordingly a lower bound on true assertion.

\paragraph{The replication misses the headline arm.} \textsc{constraint\_only} and
\textsc{swap} were measured at one seed (\S\ref{sec:exp1}). Our judgement was that
what is seed-dependent in a leak rate is the \emph{high} rate, where the model has
latitude in phrasing $S$, and that arm did replicate --- but the defended arm's
$2\%$ is single-seed and $\Lk$ has no second measurement.

\paragraph{The adversary section is underpowered where it matters.} $n=30$ per arm
cannot resolve a $7$-point difference from the permitted-information floor
(\S\ref{sec:adversary}). The null results there are not evidence of safety.

\paragraph{The side-channel test failed, and is reported as untested.} The
Experiment 2 probe for information carried around the abstraction slot returns
zero everywhere, but its own positive control also returns zero where the in-slot
probe reads $1.482$ bits (\S\ref{sec:exp2}). We therefore make no claim about the
side channel in either direction.

\paragraph{Scope.} Sixty synthetic scenarios, six domains, two model families, one
chain topology, English only. $S$ is categorical and determines the answer
exactly; a setting where $S$ shifts an answer distribution would behave
differently and we cannot say how. The decision-implied leakage figures are
properties of our generator, which we publish precisely so a reader can see what
the adversary is assumed to know.

\paragraph{One corrupt ledger line.} Our spend ledger contains a single
unparseable record from a concurrent-append race on Windows, where
\texttt{O\_APPEND} is emulated non-atomically; it is fixed with cross-process
locking, but the historical line is left in place (append-only), understating
total spend by approximately \$0.004.

\section{Conclusion}

Disclosure and use can be separated, substantially, on decision tasks: a relay
carrying only the operative constraint cuts explicit assertion of a restricted
proposition from $76\%$ to $2\%$ while losing no task utility, reaching $0.700$ of
the separability available to any non-asserting defence. On content generation the
same defence reaches $0.034$, so the result is specific to a regime where a
constraint is a sufficient summary of the fact behind it.

But the separation is of assertion, not of disclosure. The constraint that makes
the defence work carries $1.585$ of $2.585$ bits about $S$, and the answer alone
carries the same $1.585$ bits whatever the relay says --- a floor no relay-level
defence can go below while still doing the task. An LLM adversary confirms the
undefended leak decisively and cannot resolve the defended arms at our sample
size.

The practical reading is that relay-level assertion suppression is cheap,
effective, and insufficient. It defeats a detector and a casual reader; it does
not defeat an adversary who knows how the decision was made. For deployments where
that adversary is in scope, the leverage is not in the relay text but in the task
structure --- in whether the downstream agent needs to make a decision that is a
function of the restricted fact at all.

\section*{Reproducibility}

The testbed was frozen with a content hash before any paid run and verified at
every run start. All pre-registration amendments, post-freeze harness changes,
logged deviations, labelling conventions, and two withdrawn results are recorded
with reasons in the repository's running record. Total API spend was \$7.54
against a hard \$9.00 ceiling enforced from an append-only ledger. The harness
refuses to report any run in which more than $2\%$ of model calls returned no
usable text.
